PART 4: The Ocean Currents That Keep Europe from Freezing

AMOC: The Atlantic's Heat Conveyor Belt is Weakening

The Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation, known as AMOC, is like a massive conveyor belt system that carries warm water from the tropical regions near the equator all the way to Northern Europe. This ocean current system is the reason why London, England is livable and comfortable while cities at the same latitude in Canada are frozen and uninhabitable.

CURRENT STATUS OF AMOC - SCIENTISTS ARE DEEPLY CONCERNED:
  • Research has identified a clear mid-depth warming trend in the equatorial Atlantic Ocean that has stood out from natural ocean variability since the early 2000s, suggesting the AMOC likely began to weaken in the late twentieth century
  • A recent NOAA study found extensive weakening occurred in the 2000s, but has paused since the early 2010s due to a complex interaction between natural climate patterns and human-caused climate change signals

The Scientific Debate About AMOC Collapse:

  • Some computer climate models suggest AMOC collapse is unlikely to happen this century
  • Some statistical analyses using different methods predict possible collapse could occur between 2037 and 2109
  • Forty-four expert climate scientists published an open letter warning that the risk of AMOC collapse has been greatly underestimated by current models

IF AMOC COLLAPSES - THE CONSEQUENCES FOR CIVILIZATION:

Europe would experience substantial cooling with sharp drops in average winter temperatures - London could see winter cold extremes of minus 2.2 degrees Fahrenheit instead of the current mild winters.

Oslo, Norway could see temperatures as low as minus 55 degrees Fahrenheit during winter - making the city nearly uninhabitable.

Global rainfall patterns would shift dramatically - causing droughts in some regions and flooding in others.

Sea level rise would accelerate in the North Atlantic region - flooding coastal cities even faster.

Northern European agriculture would collapse completely - food production would become impossible in regions that currently feed millions.
