THE CIRCULAR CLUSTERFUCK

Welcome to the AI industry's favorite game: Vendor Financing: Extreme Edition.

Here's how it works:

  1. Nvidia invests $X in AI startup (Humans&, OpenAI, Anthropic, etc.)
  2. Startup uses that money to buy Nvidia GPUs
  3. Nvidia reports record sales and growing revenue
  4. Startup valuation increases
  5. Nvidia's equity stake increases in value
  6. Repeat until music stops

⚠️ ANALYST ESTIMATES ⚠️

NewStreet Research estimates that for every $10 billion Nvidia invests in OpenAI, they see $35 billion in GPU purchases come back.

That's a 350% return. Not from innovation. Not from product-market fit. From financing your own customers.

The Oracle Example

The circular economy reached peak absurdity with the OpenAI-Oracle deal:

  • OpenAI signs $300B cloud infrastructure deal with Oracle
  • Oracle's stock spikes 36% in one day
  • Larry Ellison's net worth increases $88 billion overnight
  • To fulfill the deal, Oracle must spend ~$40B on Nvidia chips
  • Oracle's debt-to-equity ratio: 6:1

It's money moving in circles, everyone's valuation goes up, and somehow we're all pretending this is sustainable.

"The circular economy of AI - investing hundreds of billions of dollars in companies that, in turn, use that money to buy more of Nvidia's chips."
— Ctech analysis, November 2025

Why This Matters for Humans&

Nvidia is one of Humans&'s lead investors. Humans& explicitly states they will need more compute than existing methods. Connect the dots:

Nvidia gives money → Humans& buys Nvidia GPUs → Nvidia wins either way.

It's not an investment. It's pre-paying for your own sales with extra steps.

Historical precedent: Vendor financing was a hallmark of both the dot-com bubble (2000) and the telecom bubble (2002). Lucent Technologies famously did circular deals with companies like Winstar. When the music stopped, Lucent collapsed, and millions of miles of "dark fiber" sold for pennies.

The AI bubble's circular deals make those look quaint.
