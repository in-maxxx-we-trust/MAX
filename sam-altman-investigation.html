Background & Rise to Power

Early Life

Samuel Harris Altman was born April 22, 1985, in Chicago to a Jewish family, later moving to St. Louis, Missouri. At age eight, he received his first Macintosh computer and began programming. He attended the elite John Burroughs prep school, where he publicly came out as gay and encouraged teachers to post "Safe Space" placards.

Altman attended Stanford University to study computer science but dropped out after two years, later saying he learned more playing poker with classmates than from professors. At 19, he co-founded Loopt, a location-based social networking app, which was acquired in 2012 for approximately $43 million.

Y Combinator (2011-2019)

Altman joined Y Combinator in 2011 as a part-time partner and became president in 2014, succeeding founder Paul Graham. Under his leadership, Y Combinator helped about 1,900 companies including Airbnb, Instacart, DoorDash, Reddit, and Twitch.

OpenAI Leadership (2019-Present)

In March 2019, Altman left Y Combinator to focus full-time as CEO of OpenAI, which had been co-founded in 2015 by Altman, Greg Brockman, Elon Musk, and others as a nonprofit AI research organization. Under Altman's leadership, OpenAI successfully launched ChatGPT in November 2022, positioning him as one of the leading figures of the AI boom.

03

The November 2023 Crisis

On November 17, 2023, OpenAI's board fired Altman as CEO, stating he "was not consistently candid in his communications with the board." What followed was one of the most dramatic corporate upheavals in Silicon Valley history.

The Firing
Within days, 745 of OpenAI's 770 employees signed a letter threatening mass resignations if the board didn't resign and reinstate Altman. Microsoft offered to hire Altman to lead a new AI research team. After five days of turmoil, Altman was reinstated on November 21, 2023, with a reconstituted board.

What Really Happened?

Former board member Helen Toner later explained the board fired Altman because he:

  • Withheld information about ChatGPT's release from the board
  • Failed to disclose his ownership of OpenAI's startup fund
  • Provided inaccurate information about safety measures
  • Was reported by two executives for psychological abuse and manipulation
"Former CTO Mira Murati told staffers in 2023 she didn't feel comfortable about Sam leading us to AGI. Co-founder Ilya Sutskever said 'I don't think Sam is the guy who should have the finger on the button for AGI.'"
Key Insight

The people who worked closest with Altman—his own board members and executives—didn't trust him to lead OpenAI toward artificial general intelligence. Yet after five days, he returned with more power than ever, and the board that fired him was replaced.

The Energy Power Play

Helion Energy: $1 Billion Nuclear Fusion Bet

Helion has attracted over $1 billion in funding, with more than $375 million coming directly from Altman—his largest personal investment, representing approximately 20% of his entire net worth. He has been chairman of Helion's board since 2015.

The Promise vs Reality
Promised: Commercial fusion reactor by 2028, with a 2023 agreement to supply power to Microsoft

Reality: Despite missing the 2024 deadline for demonstrating net electricity production, the company claims it's still on schedule. However, Helion has not confirmed whether it has achieved the fundamental milestone of producing more energy than required to initiate fusion.

Helion's Internal Problems

In the past year, Dr. Claire Saunders, a materials science PhD and valued Helion employee, reported harassment from her male manager, including sexual comments about forcefully inserting material into a neutron generator referred to in female terms. Helion investigated but found "insufficient evidence of discrimination." At least five women have sought remedies in the past two years, including a scientist who resigned after a harassment investigation.

On January 28, 2025 (today), Helion raised an additional $425 million at a $5.245 billion valuation—despite having no commercial product!

Oklo: Nuclear Power & Political Connections

Oklo is a startup producing small modular nuclear reactors (SMRs), where Altman served as chairman from founding until April 2025. Oklo became public in May 2024 through merger with Altman's special purpose acquisition company (SPAC) AltC, leaving the nuclear startup with over $300 million for growth.

Conflict of Interest Alert

Energy Secretary Chris Wright was also a board member of Oklo when Trump nominated him to the cabinet. Wright, CEO of Liberty Energy, sits on Oklo's board—a nuclear energy company backed by OpenAI CEO Sam Altman.

Weapons-Grade Plutonium for Altman's Company

The White House is providing plutonium to Sam Altman's company Oklo. The Department of Energy launched a request for interested parties to access up to 19 metric tons—nearly 42,000 pounds—of weapons-grade plutonium.

Oklo is one of the companies expected to receive supplies of the fissile isotope from the Department of Energy. This raises serious concerns in the scientific community about the relaxed approach to nuclear development.

Trump's Executive Orders

In January 2025, the U.S. government issued Executive Order 14156, declaring a "National Energy Emergency" to fast-track AI energy infrastructure, allowing OpenAI to bypass environmental and bureaucratic regulations, treating Stargate campuses as essential national assets.

The orders create a Department of Energy and Department of Defense partnership for at least three test reactors to achieve self-sufficiency by July 4, 2026. They also provide for reactors at military facilities by 2028.

Financial Windfalls

Through an agreement between Oklo and European firm newcleo, newcleo plans to invest up to $2 billion through an investment mechanism. Oklo's stock has soared 450% since the beginning of the year, despite the company not yet having a federal license to operate any reactor and no binding contracts to supply electricity.

Major Controversies

Sexual Abuse Allegations

In January 2025, Altman's sister Ann filed a lawsuit alleging he sexually abused her regularly between 1997-2006, when she was 3-12 years old and he was 12-21.

The Lawsuit Details
The lawsuit, filed in U.S. District Court in the Eastern District of Missouri, alleges abuse began when Ann was 3 and Sam was 12 at their family home in Clayton, Missouri. The filing claims abusive activities occurred "several times per week," beginning with oral sex and later involving penetration. The abuse included "numerous acts of rape, sexual assault, sexual abuse, molestation, sodomy, and battery," with the last instances occurring when Sam was an adult and Ann was still a minor.

The lawsuit requests a jury trial and damages exceeding $75,000. Ann claims she has experienced PTSD, extreme emotional distress, mental anguish, depression, lost wages, and "loss of enjoyment of a normal life" as a result.

Family Response

Altman, his mother, and two brothers issued a joint statement denying the allegations as "utterly untrue." The family statement said Ann has "mental health challenges" and "refuses conventional treatment," claiming she has continuously attempted to extract money from the family despite monthly stipends and other financial support.

Context

Ann first made similar claims publicly on social media in 2021. This lawsuit was filed just days before her 31st birthday—the deadline under Missouri law for child sexual abuse victims to file lawsuits.

As of December 2025, the case remains active in federal court with no public updates on hearings, motions, or resolution. In March 2025, reports emerged of a potential countersuit by Sam against Ann for defamation, though no formal filing has been confirmed.

Important Note

These are allegations in an active lawsuit. Sam Altman denies all claims. Both sides deserve to present their evidence in court. This investigation presents the facts as they are publicly known without making judgments about guilt or innocence.

Worldcoin/World Privacy Concerns

Altman co-founded Worldcoin (now World), which scans users' irises using "Orb" devices to create biometric digital IDs in exchange for cryptocurrency tokens. The project has faced massive global backlash:

  • Kenya (May 2025): High Court ordered World to delete all biometric data collected in the country, ruling it violated privacy law by using crypto payments to obtain data without valid consent
  • Portugal, Spain, Germany: All investigated or temporarily banned Worldcoin operations over data privacy concerns
  • Kenya (2023): Initially suspended operations after allegations that people who signed up never received promised money and officials were bribed

OpenAI Safety Concerns

In May 2024, former OpenAI safety researcher Jan Leike resigned, criticizing the company for prioritizing product development over safety. OpenAI was also exposed for using "extremely restrictive offboarding agreements" requiring departing employees to sign non-disparagement provisions forbidding criticism forever or lose vested equity.

"Former executives Dario and Daniela Amodei (now founders of Anthropic) described Altman's tactics as 'gaslighting' and 'psychological abuse' to those around them."

The Elon Musk Feud

Elon Musk, an OpenAI co-founder who left in 2018, has sued OpenAI twice, alleging it violated its founding mission to develop AI for humanity's benefit by pursuing profit.

In February 2025, Musk led an investor group offering to buy OpenAI for $97.4 billion, which Altman rejected, saying Musk was trying to "slow us down." A federal judge has allowed the case to proceed to trial, scheduled for 2026.

The Nonprofit Betrayal

The Original Mission

OpenAI was founded in December 2015 as a nonprofit with "the goal of building safe and beneficial artificial general intelligence for the benefit of humanity." The founding charter stated an intention to "advance digital intelligence in the way that is most likely to benefit humanity as a whole, unconstrained by a need to generate financial return."

Initial funding came from donations—$1 billion pledged by Sam Altman, Greg Brockman, Elon Musk, Reid Hoffman, Jessica Livingston, Peter Thiel, Amazon Web Services, and Infosys. However, actual capital collected significantly lagged pledges—only $130 million had been received by 2019.

The 2019 Transformation

In 2019, Altman announced that the firm's ambitions had exceeded what donations could support and that OpenAI would create a for-profit arm to attract investments. The for-profit entity would be completely controlled by its nonprofit parent, with investors' returns capped at 100 times their investment.

The Failed Restructuring Attempt

For over a year, OpenAI pursued plans to convert its core governance structure to become independent from the nonprofit. The goal: make the for-profit company independent of its current nonprofit board—the entity that fired Altman in November 2023.

Why Investors Demanded Change
The November 2023 crisis woke investors up to the idea that the interests of OpenAI's nonprofit board—to ensure AGI benefits all humanity—could conflict with their own interest in earning an outsize financial return. Investors insisted they didn't want to be in a position where their investment could be jeopardized by actions of the nonprofit board.

May 2025: The Reversal

After months of pursuing a for-profit conversion, OpenAI reversed course in May 2025. CEO Sam Altman announced the nonprofit would continue to control the company. The for-profit arm would convert to a Public Benefit Corporation (PBC) but remain under nonprofit control.

Altman cited "hearing from civic leaders and having discussions with the offices of the Attorneys General of California and Delaware" as reasons for the decision. However, he said on a call with reporters that Elon Musk's lawsuit had nothing to do with the change.

Current Structure (October 2025)

Following restructuring approved by California and Delaware regulators:

  • Nonprofit OpenAI Foundation: 26% ownership
  • Microsoft: 27% ownership
  • Employees/other investors: 47% ownership

The OpenAI Foundation retains authority to appoint the board of the for-profit OpenAI Group PBC—a mechanism designed to align strategic direction with the Foundation's charitable charter.

Unanswered Questions

Critics question whether the nonprofit can exercise meaningful control over day-to-day activities. Does the nonprofit have supervoting shares, or could new investors dilute its stake? With AGI requiring trillions of dollars according to Altman, how will the nonprofit maintain control as more capital is raised?

The Charitable Assets Debate

Legal experts and nonprofits have raised concerns about whether OpenAI is properly protecting charitable assets. If the company was built using tax-advantaged nonprofit donations and resources, some argue those assets belong to the public and shouldn't be transferred to private shareholders for profit.

ChatGPT already uses nearly 40 million kilowatts daily—enough to power the Empire State Building for 18 months—and uses over 31 million gallons of water annually just for cooling. This is likely an underestimation given complex multimodal tasks.

Altman has admitted he wants a "significant fraction" of Earth's total electricity to power AI, with OpenAI's data centers expected to consume as much power as New York City.

The "Double or Nothing" Bet

Critics call this a "double or nothing bet"—the claim that we should increase energy production and emissions now because advanced AI will eventually solve climate change, with no guarantee this technology will address challenges that are fundamentally about human behavior and political will.

"As long as we effectively subsidize fossil fuels by allowing them to use the atmosphere as a waste dump, we need policy changes, not just tech breakthroughs, to meet our climate goals."

— Climate scientist Zeke Hausfather

We Already Have the Solutions

Critics note that Altman's argument fundamentally misunderstands climate change. We already have the clean energy technology we need—nuclear, solar, wind, batteries. The challenges are sunk costs, development obstacles, political will, and inertia.

Technological advances alone can't solve climate change when we already have the tools but face challenges of economic incentives and behavioral change.

Personal Contradictions

While claiming AI will "fix the climate," Altman:

  • Drives a $15 million McLaren F1
  • Accumulated $85 million in real estate in 18 months
  • Maintains multiple luxury properties across Hawaii, San Francisco, and Napa
  • Invested hundreds of millions in energy companies that serve OpenAI's needs
The Climate Calculation

OpenAI is building infrastructure that will consume more electricity than entire countries, powered initially by natural gas, while promising that future AI will somehow solve the climate crisis caused in part by this very infrastructure.

Final Assessment

This is not a story about a visionary technologist. This is a story about the capture of power through energy, politics, and AI—under the cover of "saving humanity."

Sam Altman embodies a profound contradiction at the heart of modern tech leadership. He promises AI will "fix the climate" while building infrastructure that consumes more electricity than entire countries. He claims to want AGI to benefit all humanity while accumulating billions in private wealth. He presents himself as politically neutral while donating millions to political figures and causes.

Most critically, he has positioned himself at the center of an unprecedented concentration of power: controlling the most influential AI company in the world, while personally investing in the energy companies that will power that AI, while influencing the political system that regulates both.

"You could parachute him into an island full of cannibals and come back in five years, and he'd be the king."

The question isn't whether Sam Altman is a genius or a villain. The question is whether any one person should have this much control over technologies that will shape humanity's future—especially when their actions consistently contradict their stated values.

The Altman Files reveal not just one man's rise to power, but a system that allows such concentration of influence to occur largely unchecked, with conflicts of interest rationalized as necessary for "innovation" and environmental destruction justified as steps toward a better future.
